Three commonwealths, eight facets

Cluster every region on all eight facets at once and the European Atlas resolves into three broad, stable groups: a Latin, a Byzantine and a Hebraic commonwealth. The same three appear whether the facets are weighted by how much they agree with each other, weighted equally, or restricted to the transmitted facets alone. Plate I shows the three groups and how they were found. Plate II maps each facet separately; Plate III lets you sort every region by any of them and watch the others come apart.

    The European Atlas is three stable groups

    1. The Latin Commonwealth — 39 regions. All of Scandinavia, the British Isles, the Low Countries, the German lands, Bohemia, Slovakia, Poland, the Baltics, Hungary, Slovenia, Croatia, France, Switzerland, Austria, Iberia, all of Italy, the United States, English Canada, Québec, Australia, New Zealand and Ibero-America.

    2. The Byzantine Commonwealth — 13 regions. Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, Galicia, Serbia, Bosnia, Montenegro, Macedonia, Bulgaria, Romania, Moldova, Albania and Greece.

    3. The Hebraic Commonwealth — 2 regions. Israel and the Ashkenazim.

    These same three groups, with identical membership, appear whether the clustering uses the derived weights below, equal weights for all eight facets, or the six transmitted facets alone with ancestry and phenotype switched off. Under the derived weights the first split is now Latin against everyone else, with the Byzantine and Hebraic groups separating at the second cut. Under equal or transmitted-only weights the first split instead peels off the Hebraic pair, because those two differ from everyone on script, confession, art and legal family at once. Either way, three groups is the natural level.

    The key result is that the Latin group is exactly the set of regions coded "inside" on the Latin Roman facet. No region inside the package ended up in another group, and no region outside it ended up in the Latin group. The frontier regions — Galicia, Bosnia and Romania — all fell to the Byzantine side. So the clustering, run over all eight facets, rediscovers the Latin Roman boundary on its own. That is the strongest empirical support the atlas has for calling that facet the apex signal. One caution: Confession is essentially tied with it as an organising facet, so the honest claim is co-apex with Confession.

    Two patterns stand out. The first is that the weights come in two separate blocs rather than one hierarchy. Latin Roman, Confession, Script and now Art form a tight transmitted quartet; Art joined it on its own, correlating more strongly with Latin Roman than with any other facet. Ancestry and Phenotype form a biological pair that mostly just agree with each other. Ancestry's weight is borrowed almost entirely from Phenotype, not earned by lining up with the cultural facets, and adding Art pushed it down from first place to fifth.

    The second is that legal family is nearly weightless because it is orthogonal to everything else. Common law puts Dublin with Sydney, and civil law puts Paris with Montreal. It crosses oceans without following any other boundary.

    Finer structure

    Cut deeper, and the Latin group splits into a North Sea and Atlantic group — Scandinavia, the British Isles including Ireland, the Netherlands, north Germany, the Anglosphere and the Baltics — and a Catholic Continent: Poland through Iberia and Italy, plus Québec and Ibero-America. Adding Art moved Ireland across: its Gothic-without-Baroque profile and common law now pull it toward Britain and the Anglosphere rather than toward Catholic Europe, so the northern group is no longer purely Reformed and has been renamed. Lithuania remains the other Catholic exception, pulled north by its Baltic neighbours and its biology.

    The Byzantine group splits five ways: the Rus' lands (Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, Galicia); the South Slavs (Serbia, Bosnia, Montenegro, Macedonia, Bulgaria); the Danubian Romance pair (Romania, Moldova); Albania alone; and Greece alone.

    How stable this is

    Under 200 completely random weightings of the eight facets, including many that heavily favour biology, the exact three-way split reappeared 118 times — up from 89 of 200 before Art was added. When it breaks, the disagreements concentrate on the seam regions: Romania, Moldova, Albania, Galicia and Bosnia first, then the Rus' lands. The Latin core does not move.

    Weight biology alone and you get a completely different map. It puts Ireland, Scandinavia, Poland and Russia in one northern band, and Sardinia, the Mezzogiorno, Greece, Israel and the Ashkenazim in a southern one. That grouping matches no church, alphabet, legal system or language boundary. Biology draws north–south bands; every transmitted facet draws the Rome–Constantinople line.

    Why "commonwealth"

    The word comes from Dimitri Obolensky's The Byzantine Commonwealth (1971), which used it for peoples bound together by a shared religious, legal and literary inheritance rather than by a state or by blood. That is exactly what these clusters are. Each is named for the scriptural and legal language its inheritance was carried in — Latin, Byzantine Greek, Hebrew — which is what the clustering actually picked up, since script, confession, jurisdiction and art carried the weight. "Byzantine" holds for the eastern group even though it contains Muslim Albania and Romance-speaking Romania: under the Ottomans the Orthodox church went on administering these peoples, which the historian Nicolae Iorga called Byzance après Byzance, Byzantium after Byzantium. "Orthodox East" would fail for Albania and Bosnia; "Byzantine" does not.

      Category explanations

      The Latin juridical inheritance comes first because it is the facet the others are organised around. For each: what it measures,, and the outside case that shows what it is doing.

      The Latin juridical inheritance

      The apex signal: four medieval legal inventions that travelled together

      This is the apex cultural signal in the atlas, and it leads every plate for that reason. Three things put it there, and each can be checked on the page.

      It is the hub the other transmitted facets organise around. Sort the matrix by Confession and this column breaks into only eleven blocks against a floor of three; sort by Script and it holds to fourteen. Together with those two and Art it forms the one tight cluster on the plate, and of the four it is the one with institutional content — script, confession and the Gothic cathedral are its visible traces, the same medieval event seen from different angles. Legal family is a variation inside it: common and civil law both descend from it. The biological facets, by contrast, do not line up with it at all: sort by ancestry and it shatters into twenty-three blocks.

      It has the hardest edge and the widest reach at once. It is the one boundary on the map that is genuinely sharp — Poland from Belarus, Croatia from Serbia — and it is also the facet that crossed oceans intact, to Lima in the 1550s and later to Philadelphia, Montreal and Sydney.

      It passes Heidegger's criterion most fully. It is documentary, taught, taken up by each generation, and losable, which is exactly what a collective defined by Geschick requires.

      "Apex" here is a claim about explanatory reach — which facet best accounts for how the others fall — not a ranking of the peoples inside it. The tab reads Latin Roman because its two sources are the Latin church and the medieval reception of Roman law at Bologna. It does not mean the Roman Empire: Byzantium was the empire's direct continuation, kept Roman law in better condition than the West, and sits outside this facet. The word doing the work is jurisdiction, the thing all four criteria share: which court decides, who may appear before one, who holds authority over whom, and what a court will enforce. The four were produced by the same institutions inside about a century and a half and transmitted as a package. Where one arrived the others followed.

      Why they arrived together. Harold Berman's argument in Law and Revolution is that the founding event is the Papal Revolution — Gregory VII's break with the emperor and the Investiture Controversy that followed, roughly 1075 to 1122. The Church declared itself a jurisdiction independent of secular rulers, and to govern itself it built the first modern legal system in the West: a systematic body of law, professional courts, trained jurists, an appellate hierarchy. Secular rulers then had to build competing systems to match it. Western constitutionalism starts there, in the plain fact that for the first time there were two jurisdictions over the same people, and each could check the other.

      Load-bearing contrasts

      Arranged marriage in India. The individual-consent rule never arrived. Marriage remains overwhelmingly an alliance negotiated between lineages, with caste endogamy the organising constraint, and Indian law still recognises separate personal-law regimes by community. In much of south India cross-cousin marriage is preferred and uncle-niece marriage is practised — precisely the arrangements canon law spent four centuries prohibiting, for precisely the reason canon law prohibited them: they keep property and alliance inside the lineage.
      Parallel-cousin marriage in the Arab world. The preferred match in much of the region is a man's father's brother's daughter, the bint 'amm. This is the exact inverse of the canon-law programme and it produces exactly the outcome the canon lawyers were disrupting: dense, self-reinforcing patrilineages that remain the primary unit of trust and political loyalty.
      The waqf and the corporation. Islamic law developed a perpetual charitable endowment, the waqf, which genuinely holds property in perpetuity. But a waqf is not a legal person: it cannot sue or be sued, cannot contract, has no members, and its purpose is frozen at founding. Timur Kuran's argument in The Long Divergence is that the absence of corporate personality, not any deficit of commerce or capital, prevented durable large-scale enterprise. Everything except the persona ficta, and the firms do not form.
      The Donglin Academy, 1625. Imperial China had academies of enormous prestige older than Bologna, but not chartered corporations with jurisdiction over their own members. When the Donglin scholars became politically inconvenient, the eunuch Wei Zhongxian had the academy destroyed and its leaders killed. A European university facing a hostile prince could strike and dissolve itself into another city — Paris did exactly that in 1229 and the crown had to negotiate its return. The difference is not scholarship. It is the charter.
      The ijaza and the degree. In the madrasa tradition the licence to teach is an ijaza: a personal certificate from one named teacher to one named student, a relation between two people. A European degree is issued by a corporation and outlives everyone involved in granting it. George Makdisi showed how much the two systems share; what they do not share is the persona ficta standing behind the paper.
      Israel. The state received common law and an independent judiciary through the British Mandate and its European founders, and is coded as a frontier for a specific reason: marriage and divorce stay under the religious courts of each community, and there is no civil marriage. The corporation, the university and the binding contract arrived; the consent-before-a-common-court rule that opened the whole package did not.
      Byzantium. The best control case of all, because it had the Roman law continuously, in better condition than the West. What it did not have was a second jurisdiction. Under symphonia church and emperor were one body, so there was never an Investiture Controversy, never a rival court system, never a chancery generating law against the crown. Roman law alone is demonstrably not sufficient; the two competing jurisdictions do the work.
      Why this facet satisfies Heidegger's criterion.

      Being and Time §74 defines a collective by Geschick: a heritage handed down (Überlieferung) and taken over as a task through resolute repetition (Wiederholung). Not a property of a thing — a happening that people are inside of.

      It is transmitted, not possessed. Gratian taught at Bologna, Bologna trained the canonists, the canonists staffed the courts, the courts made the rulings, the rulings were copied into the manuals that trained the next generation. You can name the teachers.

      Membership is by taking it up. Peru enters in the 1530s by receiving the package and is fully inside; so do Philadelphia, Montreal and Sydney later. Birth confers nothing here.

      It is losable — the test ancestry and phenotype both fail absolutely. A polity that abandons judicial independence, subordinates its universities, or stops enforcing contracts against the powerful has dropped out of the transmission whatever its population's genome says.

      The honest limit. §74 shows this is a Geschick. It cannot show that it is yours — Heidegger never supplies a criterion for which heritage is properly one's own, and that vacancy is exactly what got filled by the state in 1933.

      Ancestry

      Three founding migrations, mixed in different ratios

      Almost everyone in Europe descends from the same three populations, in different proportions. Western hunter-gatherers were in place from roughly 45,000 years ago. Anatolian farmers arrived out of what is now Turkey from about 7000 BCE with agriculture, pottery and permanent settlement, and largely absorbed them. Yamnaya steppe herders arrived from the Pontic-Caspian grasslands from about 3000 BCE with Indo-European speech, horses and wheeled carts, in a movement so male-biased that in Britain the Beaker horizon replaced roughly ninety per cent of the gene pool within a few centuries.

      The mixture was essentially settled by 2000 BCE. Everything the transmitted facets describe is Iron Age at the earliest and mostly medieval, which puts this facet three millennia too early to be causing any of it.

      Load-bearing contrasts

      Punjab. Steppe ancestry is not a European property. Narasimhan and colleagues traced the same Bronze-Age steppe component into South Asia, where it is substantial in Indo-Aryan-speaking populations. A Norwegian and a Punjabi share an ancestral source that a Sardinian largely lacks. If this facet defined a people, that people would run from Bergen to Bengal.
      Ötzi and the Sardinians. The Iceman, who died in the Alps around 3300 BCE, clusters genetically with modern Sardinians. Sardinia is the closest living population to the Neolithic farmers, which makes it the most genetically continuous place in Europe — and the least steppe. Whatever "deep European" means, it points at Cagliari, not Uppsala.
      The Basques. They speak the only surviving pre-Indo-European language in western Europe and are among the most steppe-rich populations on the continent. Language and ancestry decouple completely in the one place everyone expects them to align.
      Austria and France. On this atlas's own numbers Austria is 33 steppe, 50 farmer, 17 forager; France is 32, 52, 16. The same population to within measurement error. Austria and Ireland, both West Germanic, are eight or nine points apart on two of the three components. The language boundary and the ancestry cline do not merely fail to align; they cross.
      The absence of autochthony. The finding of the whole field is repeated mass migration with near-total replacement. Nobody is rooted anywhere; everyone descends from newcomers who displaced earlier newcomers. Blood and soil requires soil, and the ancient DNA says there is none.

      Status under Heidegger's criterion

      Fails. Geschick requires a heritage that can be taken up as a task and lost by not taking it up. Nobody can take up their ancestry and nobody can lose it. Descent is a Kategorie applicable to things present-at-hand, and Dasein is not analysed through those.

      Phenotype

      Two clines that do not travel together, and one that moves within a lifetime

      The map colours this facet by light-eye frequency, because that is the axis people usually mean, and pairs it with mean adult male stature. Both vary continuously, both trend broadly northward, and the pairing is where the interest lies — the two traits come from different sources, peak in different places, and respond to different things.

      Pigmentation has no single origin. The HERC2/OCA2 variant behind blue eyes reaches its highest frequencies in the eastern Baltic, which is also where western hunter-gatherer ancestry is highest. But the hunter-gatherers were dark-skinned: the major light-skin alleles, SLC24A5 and SLC45A2, spread later with the farmers and the steppe. The two traits that combine in the popular image of a northern European arrived from opposite directions, thousands of years apart. There is no ancestral population that had both.

      Load-bearing contrasts

      The Dutch. In the mid-nineteenth century Dutch conscripts averaged around 165 centimetres and were among the shortest men in Europe. The Netherlands is now the tallest country on earth at roughly 184. Same gene pool, five generations, different nutrition and public health. Dutch emigrants to the United States stopped gaining while those who stayed kept rising. A trait that moves twenty centimetres in a century and a half is measuring circumstances rather than descent.
      The Dinaric peak. After the Dutch, the tallest populations in Europe are in Bosnia, Herzegovina and Montenegro — South Slavic, Orthodox and Muslim, on the far side of every line the nineteenth-century racial taxonomies drew. They are on the map now; switch to Phenotype and tap them. No Nordicist scheme wanted that result and none of them accommodated it.
      SLC24A5. The single largest-effect light-skin allele in Europeans is near fixation across the entire continent, Sicily and Andalusia included, and also sits at high frequency in parts of the Middle East, North Africa and South Asia. The trait most people take to be diagnostically European is neither variable within Europe nor confined to it.
      Austria, France and Denmark. Austria sits near 45 per cent light eyes, France near 30, Denmark near 80. On this axis Austria is far closer to France, across the Germanic–Romance language boundary, than to the northern end of its own language group. The pigmentation cline runs north to south and the language boundary runs east to west. They intersect; they do not overlap.
      Sardinia. Shortest population in Europe at roughly 169 centimetres, and the most genetically continuous. Continuity and stature run in opposite directions, which is the reverse of what every romantic account of rootedness predicts.
      These are the softest numbers in the atlas, by a wide margin. Pigmentation frequencies come from self-report surveys, old conscript records and small genotyping panels using inconsistent categories — "light eyes" can mean blue only or blue, green and grey together, which moves Ireland from roughly 57 per cent to roughly 85. Stature figures are recent national means from different survey years and age brackets. Read every figure as an ordering with error bars wider than the gaps between neighbours.

      Status under Heidegger's criterion

      Fails, and more decisively than ancestry. Nobody takes up a phenotype as a task and nobody loses one through neglect. Worse for the concept: the Dutch case shows the trait changing dramatically without anyone deciding anything at all, which is the opposite of a heritage taken over resolutely. A thing that shifts twenty centimetres because the milk supply improved is not a fate.

      Language

      Fichte's criterion, and what happens when you run it

      Fichte delivered the Addresses to the German Nation in Berlin in the winter of 1807 and 1808, with French troops occupying the city. The argument is almost always mis-remembered as a blood argument. It is a language argument, and he is explicit about it.

      The claim: a people that has spoken its own tongue continuously since its origin possesses an Ursprache. Every abstract term in it still traces back through living metaphor to sensuous experience all its speakers have had. A people that adopted a foreign tongue instead — his case is the Romance nations taking Latin — received its abstract vocabulary pre-formed and severed at the root. Therefore the Germans are the Urvolk, and only a people with a living root can be genuinely philosophical and therefore genuinely free.

      The tell is his own membership rule. In the Seventh Address the criterion stops being linguistic at the decisive moment: whoever believes in spirituality and its freedom, and wills its endless progress, is one of this people, wherever born and whatever tongue they speak; whoever believes in stagnation and mechanism is foreign, even if born in Germany. That is a metaphysical test, open to anyone, far closer to Heidegger's §74 than to anything in Gobineau.

      The map classifies by branch of the majority everyday vernacular today, which is why Ireland, Ulster and Scotland appear as West Germanic and Belgium, Luxembourg and Switzerland as multilingual.

      Load-bearing contrasts

      Gewissen. Fichte's argument requires German abstract vocabulary to have grown from German roots. Much of it was calqued from Latin by translators: Gewissen from conscientia, Barmherzigkeit from misericordia, Vorsehung from providentia, Eindruck from impressio, Gegenstand built to carry obiectum, Wissenschaft to carry scientia. His Ursprache is a translation.
      Yiddish. A West Germanic language, structurally closer to German than English is, written in Hebrew characters. On Fichte's own criterion the Ashkenazim are inside the German language community. The sharpest demonstration available that a linguistic criterion cannot be made to draw an ethnic boundary, and it comes from inside the framework.
      Hebrew. Nobody's mother tongue for roughly seventeen centuries, then revived as a spoken vernacular within two generations. If unbroken continuity of root is the test, Hebrew should be the deadest language on earth. It is instead the clearest case anywhere of a language taken up as a task and made living again by decision — Heidegger's criterion rather than Fichte's, and it beats Fichte's on his own ground. It is on the map now: Israel is the only Semitic cell in the atlas.
      Euskara. Basque is the only surviving pre-Indo-European language in western Europe: genuinely without an outside root, genuinely continuous. Run the Ursprache test honestly and the Urvolk of Europe are the Basques. Fichte does not consider them.
      Albanian. A branch of Indo-European entirely on its own, related to nothing else living. On a branch map it sits alone, a single-member category, which is what a genuinely isolated linguistic lineage looks like — and it belongs to a people whose other facets are thoroughly plural.
      English. Germanic in its grammar, function words and core vocabulary; roughly half Romance in its lexicon after Norman French and centuries of Latin borrowing. On Fichte's binary it is neither pure case, and it is the language of the whole Anglosphere inset.

      Status under Heidegger's criterion

      Passes, and Fichte very nearly reaches the criterion himself. A language is handed down, taken up by each generation, and genuinely losable — Ireland lost most of a vernacular within three generations and has spent a century deciding whether to take it back up, which is Wiederholung in the strict sense. Where Fichte breaks from §74 is in claiming one particular inheritance is metaphysically privileged. Heidegger's apparatus has no resources for that claim.

      Script

      The alphabet follows the missionary, not the mother tongue

      In 863 the Byzantine brothers Cyril and Methodius were sent to Moravia to preach to the Slavs in their own tongue, and built an alphabet for it — Glagolitic, from which their disciples in Bulgaria derived Cyrillic. The Latin church, meanwhile, carried the Latin alphabet with its liturgy. Where Rome won the conversion, Slavs write in Latin letters: Poles, Czechs, Slovaks, Slovenes, Croats. Where Constantinople won, they write in Cyrillic: Russians, Ukrainians, Belarusians, Bulgarians, Macedonians, Serbs.

      So script is the most visible trace of the 1054 line. It runs almost exactly along the eastern edge of the jurisdiction facet, and you can read it off a street sign from a train window. It is also, of all seven facets, the most obviously chosen: alphabets are adopted, reformed, banned and switched by decree, often within a single lifetime, while the language underneath stays the same.

      Load-bearing contrasts

      One language, two alphabets. Vuk Karadžić's reformed Cyrillic and Ljudevit Gaj's Latin alphabet were built in the first half of the nineteenth century to correspond letter for letter, and the Vienna Literary Agreement of 1850 set a common standard. The same sentence transliterates between them without loss. Croats chose one alphabet and Serbs the other along the confessional line; Serbia, Bosnia and Montenegro now use both. Here script separates what language cannot.
      Glagolitic Croatia. Catholic Croats on the Dalmatian coast and in Istria kept the old Glagolitic script and a Slavonic liturgy, with explicit papal permission from Innocent IV in 1248. The exception proves the rule: rite and script can travel apart from jurisdiction, exactly as with the Greek Catholics of Galicia.
      Romania and Moldova. Romanian was written in Cyrillic until the early 1860s, when the state switched to Latin letters as part of a deliberate re-Latinisation. Across the Prut, Soviet authorities imposed Cyrillic on the same language, renamed "Moldovan", in 1940; Moldova switched back to Latin in 1989. The same language changed alphabet three times in about 130 years and never changed its grammar.
      The Lithuanian press ban, 1864–1904. The Russian Empire outlawed Lithuanian printing in Latin letters. For forty years book smugglers carried Latin-script books over the border from East Prussia, at risk of prison and exile, and Lithuania still keeps a Book Smugglers' Day. A people defending its alphabet at personal cost is Wiederholung in its most literal form.
      Fraktur, 1941. German was printed in blackletter for four centuries, and the Nazi state promoted it as the authentic German script. In January 1941 a circular from Martin Bormann abolished it, calling it "Schwabacher Jew-letters" — a genealogy invented on the spot to justify switching to roman type, which was more legible in occupied territory. The regime most committed to the Volk's own forms abolished the Volk's own script by memo, on a fabricated pedigree.
      Turkey 1928, Albania 1908. Atatürk replaced Arabic script with Latin letters within months in 1928, with none of the Latin juridical package attached. Albania's Congress of Manastir in 1908 chose Latin letters for a Muslim-majority people with Orthodox and Catholic minorities. The alphabet is the most portable part of the inheritance and travels without any of the rest.
      Fichte's argument, applied to script.

      The case. Fichte held that a people whose language grew from its own roots keeps its concepts alive, while one that borrowed its vocabulary is severed from its own thought. Put "archive" in place of "vocabulary" and the argument transfers: a people that keeps its alphabet can read its own past directly, and one that switches is cut off from its written memory within a generation. Turkey after 1928 is the proof — Turks born a few years later could not read their grandparents' documents. An alphabet also selects a canon: Latin letters opened the Vulgate, Justinian and Aquinas; Cyrillic opened Church Slavonic and the Byzantine fathers. And script repairs Fichte's weakest point. Vocabulary is borrowed word by word, which is why the Gewissen objection sinks the Ursprache thesis, but an alphabet is adopted or refused whole, at a datable moment. It draws boundaries where language cannot — Croat from Serb, Yiddish from German — and it is defended at cost, as the Lithuanian book smugglers showed. Nobody smuggles vocabulary.

      The limits. By Fichte's own continuity test every European script fails: the Latin alphabet came to Rome from the Greeks through the Etruscans, the Greek from the Phoenicians, and Cyrillic was built for the Slavs by Greek missionaries. Fichte's Germans write in Roman letters. Script is also wildly over-inclusive — Latin letters are shared by Poles, Turks, Vietnamese and Indonesians — and switchable by decree, so if it were constitutive, Moldovans would have become a different people twice in fifty years. Fichte himself would rank it below spirit: the Seventh Address admits anyone who wills freedom, whatever tongue they speak, and so whatever letters they write.

      What survives. Script is a powerful marker and keeper of peoplehood, strongest where it divides speakers of one language and where people defend it at cost. But it is the sign of an inheritance, not its source: Croats write Latin letters because Rome converted them. That is why script travels with the Latin Roman facet as its visible trace.

      Status under Heidegger's criterion

      Passes, and more visibly than any other facet. An alphabet is handed down through schools and presses, taken up or refused by each state and each printer, and losable by decree in a single year — Romania, Moldova twice, Turkey, Germany in 1941. The Lithuanian book smugglers are the atlas's clearest case of a heritage resolutely taken over under pressure.

      Art

      Where the Gothic-to-Baroque building tradition took root

      The facet codes one thing: whether the Gothic-to-Baroque building tradition took root in a region's own churches, town halls and palaces. Five values: both Gothic and Baroque; Gothic only; Baroque only, without a medieval Gothic behind it; revival only, where the style arrived as nineteenth-century Gothic Revival; and neither. Each region is coded by its core territory, and the exceptions are named below.

      Architecture was chosen over painting deliberately. Coding sacred images directly — naturalistic figure against icon against aniconism — would largely restate Confession and count the same event twice. Buildings are a separate record: they are expensive, durable, datable, and still standing to be checked.

      Load-bearing contrasts

      Saint-Denis and Gratian, 1140. Abbot Suger's choir at Saint-Denis, the first Gothic building, went up between 1140 and 1144 — the same years Gratian was compiling the Decretum at Bologna. Gothic and canon law were born in the same decade inside the same institutional world, and the cathedral chapters that commissioned Gothic were exactly the corporate bodies canon law had just created. That is why Art correlates more strongly with the Latin Roman facet than with anything else on the plate.
      Reverse perspective. The East did not fail to reach Gothic space; it held a different idea of sacred space. Pavel Florensky's essay Reverse Perspective (1920) defends the icon's converging lines as a deliberate alternative to the Renaissance window onto the world: the image looks at the viewer rather than the viewer into the image.
      Baroque without Gothic. The eastern frontier took the later layer and skipped the earlier one. Cossack Baroque came to Kyiv through Poland-Lithuania, and Rastrelli's St Andrew's Church stands above the Dnieper. Peter and Elizabeth brought Baroque to St Petersburg. Wallachia built its own Baroque synthesis, the Brâncovenesc style, and Serbian churches in the Habsburg Vojvodina took Baroque towers and iconostases. Baroque travelled east with Polish, Habsburg and Petrine power; Gothic, which travelled with the canon-law chapters, never did.
      Transylvania, Rhodes and Kotor. Where Latin institutions reached inside an eastern state, Gothic came with them: the Saxon Black Church in Brașov, the Knights Hospitaller's Rhodes, Venetian Kotor and Perast on the Montenegrin coast. The style follows jurisdiction, not the modern border — which is why Romania, Greece and Montenegro each carry a Gothic exception inside a region coded otherwise.
      Ireland and Iceland. Both are fully inside the Latin Roman facet, and both diverge here. Ireland built Gothic cathedrals, but the Penal Laws stopped Catholic building through the Baroque centuries, leaving a Catholic country with almost no Catholic Baroque. Iceland built in turf and timber, and neither style took root in stone. Art is not a proxy for jurisdiction; it has its own material conditions.
      The Anglosphere revival. The United States, English Canada, Australia and New Zealand had no medieval Gothic to inherit, so they retrieved it: Pugin's Palace of Westminster exported, St Patrick's Cathedral in New York, Washington National Cathedral, the Gothic quadrangles of their universities. A style consciously taken up again from a past the builders never lived in is Wiederholung in stone, which is why revival gets its own value instead of being folded into "both".
      The synagogue that chose not to look like a church. When nineteenth-century Jewish communities built monumental synagogues — Dohány Street in Budapest, 1859; the Neue Synagoge in Berlin, 1866 — they mostly chose Moorish Revival, deliberately avoiding Gothic forms that would read as church. In Israel, the Crusader Gothic halls of Acre stand as ruins of a tradition that never took root.

      Status under Heidegger's criterion

      Passes. A building tradition is handed down through masons' lodges, workshops and academies, taken up or refused by each patron and each generation, and losable: the Dutch iconoclasm of 1566 stripped the churches of the Low Countries in weeks. The Anglosphere revival is the facet's clearest case of a heritage deliberately taken up again.

      Legal family

      Where law comes from, and who is allowed to make it

      A different stratum from the Latin Roman facet. That one measures what legal persons and obligations exist at all; this one measures how law gets made. In the civil law tradition the code is primary, judges apply rather than make, and earlier decisions do not bind. In the common law tradition decisions are the law: precedent binds, judges make law incrementally, and no code purports to be complete.

      From around 1500 the continent underwent the reception of Roman law. It largely stopped at the Channel, because the English royal courts had already spent three centuries building a working system out of writs and juries. Common law is a sibling inside the inheritance, not an exception to it. Mixed systems sit where the two met: Scotland, which kept a Roman-law foundation inside the United Kingdom, and Québec, which runs a French-derived Civil Code for private law and English common law for public law. The Nordic family is codified but grown from customary law rather than received Roman law, and halakha appears because the Ashkenazi row needs it: jurist-made law, with real courts, and no state behind any of it.

      Load-bearing contrasts

      Imperial China. The classical tradition set fa, penal and administrative statute from the throne, alongside li, ritual propriety, and treated litigation itself as a failure to be resolved by mediation. No autonomous legal profession, no independent judiciary, no private law of contract in the Roman sense. Law was an instrument of governance rather than a jurisdiction that could be turned against the governor.
      The PRC civil code, 2020. A modern, systematic civil code drawn from German and Soviet models, adopted deliberately and competently — without judicial independence, since the courts remain answerable to the Party. The cleanest demonstration that legal family and jurisdictional independence are separate variables.
      Islamic fiqh. Structurally closer to common law than most Europeans expect: jurist-made rather than sovereign-made, developed by scholars with real doctrinal autonomy from the ruler, through argument over cases. What it lacks is binding precedent, an appellate hierarchy, and a corporate body issuing judgements.
      Québec inside Canada. A single federal state running two legal families side by side for two and a half centuries, since the Quebec Act of 1774 restored French civil law to the conquered province. The Anglosphere inset is not one legal unit even inside one country.

      Status under Heidegger's criterion

      Passes. A legal tradition is transmitted through named institutions, taken up by each generation of practitioners as a task, and genuinely losable — a jurisdiction that stops honouring precedent has left the common law however its statutes read.

      Confession

      The quarrels that split the inheritance, and the ones that bound it

      Confession divides Europe twice, and the two divisions are not the same kind of thing. The 1054 split turned on papal primacy and the Filioque — whether the Creed should say the Spirit proceeds from the Father and the Son. That line maps almost exactly onto the eastern edge of the jurisdiction facet and onto the script boundary, because all three are the same event seen from different angles.

      The Reformation is a different order of quarrel: scripture alone against scripture plus tradition, faith alone against faith formed by charity, two sacraments against seven. Arguments about how grace reaches a person, conducted in Latin by men trained in the same universities. Protestant polities kept canon-law marriage structure, corporate personality, the chartered university and the binding promise. The Greek Catholic value exists because of the Union of Brest in 1596: Byzantine rite, Latin jurisdiction. And the southeast corner of the map carries Islam — Albania's plurality and Bosnia's Bosniaks, the legacy of four centuries of Ottoman rule.

      Load-bearing contrasts

      Christ, across three traditions. For Christianity, Jesus is God incarnate, the second person of the Trinity, crucified and risen. For Islam, 'Isa is a prophet and the Messiah, born of a virgin, honoured — but not divine, and not crucified, since the Qur'an says it was made to appear so; and to say God has a son is shirk, the gravest sin. For Judaism, he is not the messiah, because the messianic age plainly did not arrive, and a triune God cannot be reconciled with the Shema. Three traditions sharing Abraham, prophets, strict monotheism and most of a moral vocabulary, dividing absolutely on one person. Nothing about ancestry, phenotype, language or legal family predicts which side of that line a population falls on — and Bosnia holds all three sides inside one small country.
      One clause in the Creed. The Filioque is a single word. It contributed to a schism that has run a thousand years and that this atlas registers as its hardest boundary. That a disagreement of this size can be load-bearing while ancestry differences of forty percentage points are not is the central finding of the whole exercise.
      Bavaria and Saxony. Two populations no genetic method can distinguish, indistinguishable on phenotype, under the same jurisdiction, legal family and script, speaking dialects of the same language. What divides them arrived in 1517, is entirely a matter of doctrine, and has structured five centuries of politics, marriage and war. It left no genetic trace at all.
      Ulster. Same island as Dublin, same language, same script, same common law, same ancestry. The unit on the map is coloured "split" because the one thing that divides its people from each other is the confessional line — and it was enough to sustain thirty years of violence within living memory.

      Status under Heidegger's criterion

      Passes. Confession is handed down through named institutions, taken up or not by each person and each generation, and losable in the most direct way any facet here is losable. It also shows the vacancy in §74 as clearly as anything could: the criterion cannot tell a Bavarian which of the two inheritances available in 1517 was properly his.

      Selected insights

      Each pair below is united by at least one facet and split by at least one other. Tap the names on the map to check them.

      The decisive row is Sardinia beside the Ashkenazi diaspora. On ancestry they are neighbours, and on phenotype they are close too. On every transmitted facet — language, script, jurisdiction, legal family, confession — they are as far apart as two European-adjacent populations get. The two biological facets are real signals that predict almost nothing anyone has ever organised a life around.
